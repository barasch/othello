Othello Play

Reference

Rules & strategy

A short field guide.

Rules

Black moves first. Place a disc on an empty square so that one or more straight lines—horizontal, vertical, or diagonal—run from the new disc to another disc of your color with only opposing discs between. Every opposing disc in those lines turns to your color.

If you have no legal move, you pass. The game ends when neither player can move. The player with more discs wins.

Strategy

Corners cannot be recaptured. They are normally the most valuable squares. Squares immediately beside an empty corner are dangerous because they can give the corner away.

Disc count is often misleading until late in the game. Prefer moves that preserve your options and restrict your opponent’s mobility. Stable edge discs can be useful, but taking an edge too early may still concede a corner.

Near the end, count the empty squares and consider move order. A forced pass changes that order; a locally attractive capture may leave the final move to your opponent.

Openings

Standard begins with four discs. XOT instead chooses one balanced eight-move position at random from the large opening list compiled by Matthias Berg and Borja Moreno. If that sequence would leave White to move when the player chose Black, its final move is omitted.

For the formal rules, see the World Othello Federation. The computer player is adapted from Roemer B. Lievaart’s 1987 IOCCC entry. XOT positions come from the large Berg–Moreno opening list.
